# Round 3 advisory: shared-observer identifiability on the existing task

Role: advisory, non-blocking, no live authority; main owns implementation, integration and acceptance.
Base fa961c38. Development data only; no closed-loop runs, no controller/test/config edits, no commits.
This directory holds read-only postprocessing (`scripts/`) and derived numbers (`data/`). The per-tick
cache is `/tmp/ynv2` (regenerated from the retained receipts by
`report/yield-normal-v2/discussion/scripts/extract_ticks.py`; not retained). All 14 receipt hashes were
re-verified against the round-2 manifest before use (`data/manifest.json`).

Evidence classes used throughout:
[S] structural (task kinematics, analytic identities); [D] data from retained closed-loop receipts, used as
evidence about the observation model on trajectories generated by other estimators; [R] open-loop
replay, mechanism only; [P] closed-loop prediction from a steady-state model, falsifiable; [I] ideal-force
assumption of the plant's Coulomb contact. None of these is closed-loop validation.

## 0. Answers

1. **Identification beyond a frozen prior is meaningful only as prior-error correction, and on this task
   only the along (x) component is identifiable from motion.** [S][D] The true normal deviates from the
   approach by a 1.75 deg sinusoid at the task fundamental (x RMS 1.35 deg, y RMS 0.15 deg, max rotation
   rate 0.26 deg/s). Following it is worth at most 0.6 to 0.8 deg RMS, at the observer jitter floor and below
   0.1 N of force-axis leak. The lateral (y) tangent direction is excited only in two 7.5 s windows where the
   reference speed is 2.00 to 2.07 mm/s, on the 2 mm/s excitation gate; in the honest-frame closed loop the
   actual speed there is 1.5 to 1.8 mm/s, the gate is open 0 to 5 percent of the time, and the gated
   direction Gramian carries 1.8 to 4.1 s of lateral information per 63 s period. [R] A 10 deg lateral prior
   error is left at 6 to 10 deg after 30 s by the motion-tangent residual at gamma 0.3 or 1.0.
2. **Smallest matrix: 8 closed-loop cells on one material, 9 with term attribution (section 3).** Three
   initial states (approach, x-tilt, y-tilt) on the nominal scenario separate prior correction from
   surface following; the two existing intervention scenarios with the approach prior separate the two
   contamination channels; two frozen-tilt cells and one frozen tangent cell are the references.
3. **The coplanarity term is justified against normal motion but not yet against the protocol's own
   tangent push.** [S][I] f x v = N (s + mu a)(n x t): normal velocity a cancels identically. [D] Across
   248 k gated ticks the true-normal coplanarity residual grows from 0.0011 to 0.006 RMS (p99 0.0034 to
   0.018) as |a|/s goes from below 0.05 to above 1.2, three times smaller with the raw 4 ms force, so the
   growth is controller filter lag, bounded at about 1 deg equivalent. [D] In the tangent-push cells the
   residual is 0.24 to 0.26 RMS (14 to 15 deg equivalent), entirely from the external push, because the
   actual sliding direction deviates a median 17 deg from the push direction. [R] Fed to a coplanarity update
   at gamma 0.3 this moves the estimate 5 to 8 deg within the 10 s hold. The two residuals therefore have
   complementary contamination (motion residual by the normal push, coplanarity residual by the tangent
   push). Whether an honest frame reduces the 17 deg deviation is unknown and only a closed-loop tangent
   cell can answer it.

## 1. Structural observability of the existing task [S][D]

Reference kinematics (Task: x = 40 sin 0.1t mm, y = 10 sin 0.2t mm): |v|^2 = 16 c^4 + 4 (mm/s)^2 with
c = cos 0.1t. The speed minimum is exactly 2.0 mm/s, reached when the direction is purely lateral.

Mechanism: with an honest frame the friction offset mu N / Kp (5 to 13 mm) rotates through the slow turn,
and its rate of change subtracts from the reference progress. The estimator therefore never sees the
lateral sweep. The legacy frame absorbed friction into the normal and kept the speed above the gate.
Consequence: on this task, lateral prior error is structurally unobservable to the motion-tangent
residual in the honest-frame closed loop; along prior error is well excited (28 to 36 information seconds).

Surface content: x component 1.35 deg RMS, 1.78 deg max; y component 0.15 deg RMS; dominant term
1.75 deg at 0.0159 Hz (the task fundamental); rotation rate 0.14 deg/s RMS, 0.26 deg/s max. First-order
tracking residual amplitude at the fundamental: 0.9 deg at an effective rate of 0.17/s (gamma 0.3 times the
gate fraction), 0.5 deg at 0.3/s, 0.16 deg at 1.0/s. [R] Untilted replays on the forceoff nominal cells give
0.56 to 0.94 deg RMS (last 10 s) for the two-residual observer versus the 1.29 to 1.36 deg prior. The
improvement is real but at the scale of the jitter floor and of a 0.1 N force-axis leak; it does not change
task metrics and cannot serve as the discriminator.

## 2. The coplanarity term [S][I][D][R]

Identity in the plant's contact model: with f = N n_out - mu N r(v_t) and v = v_t + a n,
f x v = N (s + mu a)(n x t). The normal velocity cancels; the residual r2 = n_hat . (f x v)/|f x v| observes
the lateral (binormal) error component and the update is sign-invariant. Degeneracy requires a = -s/mu
(-30 mm/s stiff, -11 mm/s compliant), beyond the 3 mm/s normal cap. Assumptions: contact normal force along
the true normal, friction anti-parallel to the actual tangential velocity, no force outside the (n, v)
plane, no sensor bias, negligible force-filter lag relative to direction changes.

Data test of the a-immunity, all non-tangent cells, contact and excitation gated, true normal:

| abs(a)/s bin | ticks | filtered RMS | filtered p99 | raw RMS | raw p99 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 to 0.05 | 140045 | 0.0011 | 0.0034 | 0.0003 | 0.0007 |
| 0.1 to 0.2 | 31834 | 0.0027 | 0.0109 | 0.0008 | 0.0034 |
| 0.3 to 0.5 | 18060 | 0.0035 | 0.0124 | 0.0014 | 0.0051 |
| 0.5 to 0.8 | 6894 | 0.0049 | 0.0180 | 0.0021 | 0.0097 |
| above 1.2 | 2325 | 0.0060 | 0.0160 | 0.0017 | 0.0051 |

The residual grows tenfold with normal motion but stays below 0.02 (about 1 deg equivalent at p99, 0.3 deg
at RMS), and the raw-force residual is three times smaller, so the growth is the 20 ms controller filter
lagging the friction direction, not the normal velocity itself. This is evidence about the observation
model on closed-loop trajectories, not closed-loop validation of the term as a driver.

Tangent-push cells (legacy frame, 20.5 to 30.5 s, gated):

| Cell | residual RMS | external-push part | contact part | equivalent tilt | push vs actual v_t angle | frame error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| legacy stiff tangent | 0.242 | 0.242 | 0.002 | 14.0 deg | 17.5 deg | 22.5 deg |
| legacy compliant tangent | 0.261 | 0.260 | 0.002 | 15.1 deg | 16.9 deg | 13.1 deg |

The push follows the reference tangent while friction follows the actual tangential velocity; the two
differ by 17 deg here. Part of that deviation is the legacy frame pull, part is yield and path-recovery
motion; the split is not recoverable from these receipts. [R] Replaying the coplanarity update (gamma 0.3,
no cone gate) on these cells moves the estimate by 4 to 7 deg in x and 2 to 6 deg in y during the
hold; the motion-tangent update alone stays within 1.4 deg (its energy ratio there is 0.003 to 0.01).

Complementary contamination, from the receipts:

| Residual | contaminated by | magnitude seen | immune to |
|---|---|---|---|
| motion tangent n.v | normal push and release (energy ratio 0.04 to 0.22 during hold, 0.47 after release in NO-v2) | 2 to 2.3 deg drift at gamma 0.3 open loop; 39 deg closed loop in NO-v2 | force direction, force bias |
| coplanarity n.(f x v) | tangent push whose direction differs from actual motion | 5 to 8 deg open loop at gamma 0.3 | normal velocity, load, mu value |

Real-hardware caveat outside these data: a constant force bias b (gravity compensation, drift) enters r2
as b_perp/|f|; 0.2 N is 2.3 deg. The motion residual is bias-immune. Neither channel is identified on the
robot.

Verdict on question 3: the term remains justified against servo and force-loop normal motion, by identity
and by data. Its unresolved risk is the protocol's own in-plane push. Main rejected a cone gate as
arbitrary; the alternative is to measure the closed-loop tangent cell and let its outcome decide.

## 3. Smallest diagnostic matrix

Fixed: DSFC mechanics, common outer loop, QP, plant (2 ms, 8 substeps), full cycle, cold preparation,
protocol scenarios unchanged, physical approach and surface unchanged. Only the estimator's initial state
and estimator parameters vary. One material first; both only where mu or stiffness enters the hypothesis.

Tilt magnitude, a scale rather than a criterion: it must exceed the prior band (1.8 deg max) and the jitter
floor (0.5 to 0.9 deg RMS) by enough to resolve, so at least about 5 deg; and the feedforward normal leak
s_max sin(tilt) must leave the 3 mm/s normal cap room for force regulation, which excludes the compliant
friction angle (21.8 deg leaks 1.66 mm/s, 55 percent of the cap). Empirical anchors are the legacy frame
error (6.2 deg stiff, 17 deg compliant) and the stiff friction angle (8.5 deg). Ten degrees satisfies both
bounds on both materials, leaks 0.78 mm/s, and matches the round-2 replays; use the same magnitude for
every tilted cell so the comparison is paired.

| Cell | observer | initial state | scenario | separates | primary statistic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | frozen (forceoff) | x-tilt | nominal | cost reference | path RMS, load, attitude vs [P] table |
| C2 | frozen | y-tilt | nominal | cost reference | same |
| C3 | candidate | x-tilt | nominal | along prior correction | error component along x vs time; y leak |
| C4 | candidate | y-tilt | nominal | lateral prior correction | error component along y vs time; gate-open per window |

| C6 | candidate | approach | sustained_release_normal | motion-residual contamination | 2 s energy ratio (n_hat.v)^2 over |P v|^2; n_hat excursion after release; existing forceoff normal is the reference |
| C7 | candidate | approach | sustained_release_tangent | coplanarity contamination | r2 during hold; angle push vs actual v_t; n_hat excursion |
| C8 | frozen | approach | sustained_release_tangent | reference for C7 (none exists for an honest frame) | same as C7 |
| C9 | candidate minus one term | y-tilt | nominal | term attribution | as C4 |

C9 is the other variant of whatever main runs as the candidate: if the candidate is motion-only, C9 adds
the coplanarity term; if the candidate has both, C9 sets its gain to zero. Eight cells decide, nine
attribute. Both materials: 16 (18). The lateral result is expected to be material-sensitive through the
gate (compliant has 1.8 s of lateral information, stiff 4.1 s), so if only one material is run choose
stiff_low_mu, where NO-v2 failed and where the friction angle leaves a 10 deg tilt separable.

Falsifiable predictions, from [R] at gamma1 = gamma2 = 0.3, cap 0.05 rad/s, 2 mm/s gate, no residual or
cone gates, and from [P]:

- C1, C2 [P]: with a frozen y-tilt of 10 deg and sliding along x the true load is 5.08 N and the path
  offset 9.0 mm stiff (15.3 mm compliant) against 5.0 mm (11.8 mm) untilted; with a frozen x-tilt the
  offset alternates between 2.2 mm (sliding +x, the tilt cancels friction) and 12.8 mm (sliding -x) on stiff
  and the load between 4.97 and 5.19 N; attitude error stays about 10 deg. If the tilted cells do not show
  these, the tilt did not reach the force axis and the cell must be re-read before any observer inference.
- C3 [R]: motion-only reduces the x component from 10.9 to 1.4 deg within 10 s (stiff) and 4 deg (compliant),
  and leaks 2 to 3 deg into y that persists because y is unobserved; with both terms the y leak stays within
  1.2 deg and decays, and the total falls below 2 deg at 7.7 s (stiff) or 11.9 s (compliant). The closed loop adds the
  force-axis and feedforward coupling absent from the replay; a faster or slower along correction is
  informative, a lateral leak that does not appear would mean the closed loop supplies lateral excitation
  the open loop lacks.
- C4 [R][S]: motion-only leaves the y component at 6 to 10 deg through the first 30 s at gamma 0.3 and at
  gamma 1.0, with the gate open 0 to 5 percent inside the lateral windows; the coplanarity term (alone or
  combined) brings it below 2 deg in 7 to 11 s. If motion-only corrects the lateral tilt in closed loop,
  the tilt itself generated lateral motion through the phantom force (about 9 mm of lateral offset), and
  the gate-open fraction per window will show it. If the combined observer does not correct it, the
  coplanarity channel is contaminated in closed loop by something the receipts did not contain.
- C5 [R]: tracking gain of the x excursion between 0.5 and 0.9; RMS 0.5 to 0.9 deg against 1.36 deg
  frozen; task metrics within the cell-to-cell scatter of the forceoff cells. A gain near zero means the
  observer is effectively frozen; a gain above one or a worse path RMS means it is following something other
  than the surface.
- C6 [R]: motion-only accumulates 2 to 2.3 deg, mostly lateral, after the release; both terms end below
  1 deg after a transient of up to 1.8 deg on the compliant cell; the closed-loop energy ratio decides which regime applies (it reached 0.47 after release in NO-v2
  and 0.001 to 0.04 in forceoff). Contact loss or a load spike here reproduces the NO-v2 failure at the new
  gain and is a rejection, whatever the estimate did.
- C7 versus C8 [D][R]: the deciding unknown is the angle between the push and the actual tangential
  velocity under an honest frame. If it stays near the legacy 17 deg, a coplanarity-bearing observer will
  move 6 to 8 deg during the hold and its path recovery will differ from C8; if the honest frame sliding
  follows the push, r2 stays near the nominal 0.0003 to 0.003 and the term is clean under this protocol. No
  outcome here is predicted with confidence; that is the point of running it.
- C9: whichever term is removed, the y-tilt cell should reproduce the corresponding row above. If both
  variants correct the lateral tilt equally, the correction is coming from closed-loop lateral motion and
  the coplanarity term is not needed for this task; if neither does, the lateral component is declared
  unobservable on this task.

Implementation notes for main (no code proposed):

- The estimator runs during the 1 s entry: in the forceoff stiff nominal receipt 45 percent of the 500
  entry ticks applied a motion update, the first at tick 1. Log the estimate error at the first controller
  tick and at PATH time zero separately; the initial-state parameter is already in the identity.
- Report the error as two signed tangent-plane components (base x, base -y) per second, not only the angle;
  the mechanisms differ by axis and the replay JSON uses that decomposition.
- Record the gate-open fraction per window as a primary diagnostic; the lateral result hinges on it. Do
  not lower the 2 mm/s gate for this experiment: the reference minimum is 2.0 mm/s, and the motion-residual
  contamination ratio a^2/s^2 is largest exactly at low speed.
- The frozen-tilt predictions above are the check that a tilted cell exercised the force axis as intended.

## 4. What this round does not establish

- Open-loop replays use trajectories produced under a different estimate. Force-axis rotation, path
  projection, feedforward normal leak (0.78 mm/s at 10 deg), orientation retargeting and any offset
  transient are absent. They support mechanism and give the predictions above; they are not closed-loop
  results, and closed-loop coupling can change both convergence rates and contamination.
- The plant's contact is ideal Coulomb with a regularised direction; coplanarity holds there by
  construction for nominal and normal-push cells. No out-of-plane push exists in the protocol; that gap is
  recorded, not addressed, because adding a scenario is a protocol change.
- Orientation is decoupled from contact dynamics in the plant, so the attitude cost of a wrong prior has no
  feedback here; on hardware a tilted tool changes the contact patch.
- No sensor bias, drift, stick-slip or tool compliance is modelled; the force-geometry channel is the one
  exposed to these.
- All numbers are development data; nothing here is holdout, hardware or acceptance evidence, and no
  observer wins on this basis.
